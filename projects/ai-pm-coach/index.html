AI PM Coach

An AI coach for product managers. You talk through a product problem out loud, AI personas or a whole panel push back on your answer, and an AI judge scores it on five PM competencies and tells you exactly what to fix.

Try the prototype

Works best in Chrome or Edge, where you can answer by voice. Without an AI key you still get live transcription and delivery feedback. Add a key in Settings, Groq's free tier works, to unlock follow-ups and scoring.

The problem

A PM's core skill is judgment: which problem to solve, what to cut, and how you'll know it worked. But the feedback loop on judgment is painfully slow. Engineers find out in minutes, from tests and code review, whether their work holds up. PMs make a call, wait a quarter for it to ship, and then see results tangled up with a dozen other factors. By then it's hard to tell whether the thinking was good or the timing was lucky.

For AI products the loop is slower still, because the playbook is still being written. Questions like how to evaluate a model, what guardrails an agent needs, or when it should act without asking don't have years of experience behind them yet. Most PMs get a handful of real chances a year to work through them, and every one counts.

Who it's for

Product managers who want to sharpen their product thinking, especially on AI products, where questions about evals, agents, guardrails, and cost now sit alongside classic product design, strategy, metrics, and leadership. It's also useful practice before any high-stakes conversation where you'll be asked to think on your feet, interviews included.

How it works

  1. Pick the topic. Product Design, AI Product, Strategy, Analytics and Metrics, or Behavioral. Choose a question or let it surprise you, and choose a realistic session or practice with hints.
  2. Pick a persona, or a panel. Six personas, from a friendly senior PM to an exacting product leader, a staff engineer, and a principal designer, each with their own voice and focus.
  3. Answer out loud. The persona reads the question aloud. Your answer is transcribed live, with meters for time, pace, and filler words as you speak.
  4. Get probed. Up to three follow-ups, each aimed at the weakest part of what you just said. Ask a clarifying question and the persona answers with concrete assumptions.
  5. Get judged. An overall score out of 10, a verdict against the bar, five competency scores with quoted evidence and a tip each, an outline of an exceptional answer, a stronger opening, and one drill for next time.
  6. Ask the judge. "Why did I only get a 3 on user insight?" Ask by voice or by typing, and the judge answers from your transcript.
  7. Watch yourself improve. A score trend, a competency profile, and a one-tap drill for your weakest competency.

Design choices

55practice questions across five topics
6personas, one-on-one or as a panel of two or three
5competencies scored, each backed by a quote from your answer
  • A judge that doesn't flatter. Every score uses fixed anchors, where 3 means "meets the bar" and 5 is rare, and every score has to quote the transcript. A coach that inflates scores is worse than none.
  • Delivery is measured, not guessed. Pace, filler words, hedges, signposting, and long pauses are counted in the browser from your transcript and timing. The judge receives the numbers rather than estimating them.
  • Personas with a point of view. The engineer asks about latency, data, and build cost. The designer asks about flows, edge cases, and accessibility. On a panel, the AI picks who speaks next and spreads the questions around, like a real review.
  • Useful with no AI at all. Without a key you still get transcription, playback of your recording, delivery analysis, scripted follow-ups, and reference outlines. The AI adds probing and scoring on top.
  • Your model, your data. Bring OpenAI, Azure OpenAI, Groq, OpenRouter, Together, Hugging Face, or a local model through Ollama or LM Studio. The key lives only in memory and is never saved, and your progress stays in your browser unless you export it.
